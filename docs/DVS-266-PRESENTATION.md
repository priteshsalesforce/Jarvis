## Slide 4 — Customer Value & Job(s) To Be Done

**What is the value to the customer?**
- **Employees** get time back — less portal-hopping, less context-switching. One morning brief instead of 13 interruptions.
- **The business** sees fewer routine HR/IT tickets reach the service desk, because Jarvis completes them conversationally.
- **Managers** spend time on exceptions and team readiness, not repetitive approvals.
- **Salesforce** owns the employee *action* layer — the surface expands from HR/IT Service into broader employee work over time.

**What are the Job(s) to be Done?**
- *When my day gets disrupted,* help me reshuffle quietly so I don't lose 20 minutes apologizing and rescheduling.
- *When messages pile up,* draft the polite replies in my voice so I just review and approve — in one batch, not one-by-one.
- *When an action affects other people,* make sure it waits for my explicit OK, and let me undo anything.
- *Every morning,* give me one brief of what actually needs me — and don't add to the noise.

> **Differentiation (be ready for it — Deval flagged competitive overlap):** Jarvis is a *design vision built on Agentforce*, not a competing product. It complements Employee Service (which answers questions) by being the **cross-system action layer** — the contribution is the **interaction model**: tiered trust, batch approval, rank-aware prioritization, and a reversible audit trail. Keep this in speaker notes; raise it proactively if the room goes there.
